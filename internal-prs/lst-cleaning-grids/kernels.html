LST duplicate removal on eta-phi grids: per-step changes, timing and memory

Before = master 5b4335d798f; after = the same master with the change applied (three commits, head eb5e54e3d45). The change speeds up the seven duplicate-removal and cross-cleaning steps of LST. Each step compared every pair of objects; now it visits only the objects that can pass its cuts, found through eta-phi grids and hit buckets. Per step: the kernels before → after and their time; then the totals and one memory table.

Method: LST standalone, built in the CMSSW_20_1_0_pre4 environment, HLT settings (pT cut 0.8 GeV), CPU 32 streams, GPU (NVIDIA L40) 1 stream, mean of 2 rounds (they agree within 4.7% for every value above 0.002 ms). Kernel times come from an instrumented build, not part of the change, that waits after each kernel; a step time also includes its allocations, memsets and host synchronizations. On the CPU, the instrumented and the plain builds give identical track candidates. Memory: LST's own per-event allocation report (CPU, 1 stream), with the new buffers added to it in the instrumented build. Samples:

  • ttbar: the standalone PU200 ttbar file, 175 events. Its pLSs come from offline seeds, about 21.6k per event.
  • QCD PU200: 200 events of RelValQCD_Pt_1800_2400_14 PU200 (D121), run through the Phase-2 HLT menu (CMSSW_20_1_X_2026-10-27-2300) into a tracking ntuple. Its pLSs are the HLT pixel tracks that hltInputLST reads, about 1.8k per event.
  • In the totals only: ttbar PU200 through the same HLT chain (200 events) and QCD high-pT jets without pileup (the standalone jets file, 200 events).

Each new grid or hit-bucket list is built in three launches: a count pass, ScanCellCounts, then a fill pass. The count and fill passes are the same kernel, which therefore runs twice per event (marked "2 calls"; the time is for both calls). ScanCellCounts runs once per step. Every other kernel runs once per event.

Shared code

  • EtaPhiGrid (new EtaPhiGrid.h): eta-phi cells at least as wide as the step's window, so for finite eta and phi the 3x3 cells around an object hold all its partners inside the window.
  • ScanCellCounts (same file): one block; turns the per-cell counts into cell offsets between the count and fill passes. Its seven calls take 0.02 ms/event on the CPU and 0.08 on the GPU, included in the rows above.
  • Hit buckets (new HitBuckets.h): 8192 buckets by the low 13 bits of the hit index, so two objects that share a hit are in the same bucket.
  • On the serial CPU backend, which runs one thread per block, the launches sized by modules or objects use a single block.

The rest of these three stages keeps its time: CreatePixelQuintupletsFromMap and CreatePixelTripletsFromMap within 2.1% (at most 0.8% slower), and within 0.04 ms/event the two compactions and, in the TC stage, RemoveDupQuadrupletsBeforeTC, CountSurvivingTCs, AddpT5asTrackCandidate, AddpT3asTrackCandidates, AddT5asTrackCandidate, AddT4asTrackCandidate and AddpLSasTrackCandidate. On the GPU in QCD PU200 most of the remaining TC stage is RemoveDupQuadrupletsBeforeTC (10.6 ms/event), which the change does not touch. The other LST stages, from the hits to the T4s, keep their times within 0.7%, except the hit loading on the CPU, which moves by up to 3.6%; the change does not touch those stages.

Slower on the GPU with the HLT pixel seeds

Three steps take longer than before in these numbers in QCD PU200: pLS cross cleaning (0.042 → 0.080 ms/event), pT3 duplicates (0.019 → 0.053) and pT3 cross cleaning (0.019 → 0.045). The same holds in the HLT-chain ttbar (0.040 → 0.072, 0.016 → 0.049, 0.018 → 0.044) and in the jets without pileup (0.014 → 0.072, 0.012 → 0.052, 0.012 → 0.045). With about 1.8k pLSs, 430 pT3s and 1.7k pT5s per event in QCD PU200, the count, scan and fill launches of a step take longer than the old loop over the few pairs. These numbers put a wait after every launch, which charges each extra launch its full latency. Without the instrumentation, the pT3 stage, which holds one of the three steps, goes 0.94 → 0.95 ms/event in QCD PU200 (+1.2%, in each of the three rounds), while the pT5, pT3 and TC stages together go 15.52 → 12.23 ms/event and the whole event 37.85 → 34.55 ms. With the instrumentation the pT3 stage goes 0.97 → 1.00 ms/event in QCD PU200, 0.14 → 0.17 in the HLT-chain ttbar and 1.00 → 1.03 in the jets.

LST's peak allocation per event does change in ttbar. The peak is in the TC stage, after the TC collection is allocated, and the T4 cross-cleaning buckets are live there. So it rises by 0.16–0.23 MB in every ttbar event: 17.40 (24.75) → 17.60 (24.98) MB. In QCD PU200 the peak is reached outside the cleaning steps, so it is unchanged in every event (200 checked): 24.57 (45.49) MB in the first 100 events.

What does not change:

  • The sizes of every stored collection, from the hits to the TCs, and of the pT5, pT3 and T4 creation buffers, in every event (100 ttbar, 200 QCD PU200). The TC counts by type are identical in every event too.
  • The track candidates: on the CPU, identical in every event of all four samples (pt, eta, phi and type of every TC). On the GPU, two runs of one and the same build already differ, in about 0.01% of the TCs in ttbar and 0.14% in QCD PU200 (checked for both before and after); before and after differ by the same amount.
